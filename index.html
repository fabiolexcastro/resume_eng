Who I am

A geographer who likes sharing what he knows through video tutorials

I studied Geography at Universidad del Valle and then completed an MSc in Geographic Information Science at the University of Salzburg. For more than ten years I've worked at an agricultural research center, building climate and crop models alongside teams from very different disciplines.

Along the way I found that what I enjoy most is passing on what I learn. I record video tutorials, teach courses, and spend a good part of my time explaining how a raster works, or how a species distribution model is built.

It all points in the same direction: getting science out of the PDF and into the places where decisions about the land are actually made.

Research

Two studies, one pattern: life is moving up the mountain

Much of what I do ends up in peer-reviewed papers. In these two, with different teams, the same thing showed up: as temperature rises, both crops and wild species have to look for higher ground to survive.

First author

Rice climbs to higher ground in Colombia

We mapped where irrigated rice can be grown today and towards 2050 under a severe climate scenario (RCP 8.5), using niche models built with MaxEnt and 32 global climate models. The result: suitable area could shrink by roughly 60% — from 4.4 to 1.8 million hectares.

The lowlands would be hit hardest, while mid-elevation valleys such as the upper Cauca and the Patía would gain suitability. Unlike temperate countries, which can shift their crops towards cooler latitudes, in the tropics rice has no such option: it has to move uphill.

Castro-Llanos, Hyman, Rubiano, Ramirez-Villegas & Achicanoy (2019) · Mitigation and Adaptation Strategies for Global Change

Co-author

Refuges for dry forest orchids

With a team, we studied 12 orchid species typical of the Colombian tropical dry forest and modeled their habitat towards 2030 and 2050. We saw their suitable range shift from the lowlands (0–1000 m) up to mid elevations (1300–1700 m), shrinking sharply along the way.

On that basis we proposed altitudinal migration corridors: connecting the remaining lowland forest patches with mid-elevation areas that act as thermal refuges, so that plants and animals have somewhere to move to. A concrete conservation strategy in the face of climate change.

Reina-Rodríguez, Rubiano, Castro-Llanos & Soriano (2017) · Lankesteriana
